# M3 — Narrative (the arc the v2/v3 paper tells)

This is the intellectual through-line for the revision — how the story tightened from v1
to v2/v3 and where each result sits. It is guidance for the author, not paper prose.

## One sentence
*A leakage-audited, pre-registered benchmark shows that on the powered target
well-specified physics (ETAS) decisively out-forecasts machine learning in the Sea of
Marmara, that a machine-learning "win" from a GNSS deformation channel was a placebo-level
artifact, and that the durable operational product is a standing hazard map with honestly
bounded timing.*

## The three-act structure

**Act 1 — The trap the field keeps falling into.** Short-term ML earthquake forecasts are
haunted by data leakage: features that peek at the target inflate apparent skill. v1's
contribution was a *machine-checkable* causality gate (truncated-catalogue self-test). v2/v3
adds the second half of the credibility story — a *statistical* gate. Point estimates are
not verdicts; we pre-register a stationary block bootstrap over whole 30-day windows and
declare a winner only when both information gain and PR-AUC intervals exclude zero. The
paper's spine is methodological honesty made mechanical: `claims.json` is generated by the
referee, and the prose is forbidden from out-running it.

**Act 2 — When the test has power, physics wins.** v1 could not separate ML from ETAS at
M≥3.5 and left it there. The powered M≥3.0 target (592 vs 167 positives) resolves the tie:
the cascade, sv-ETAS, Mizrahi, and first-generation models form one inseparable cluster
that beats the ML hybrid on *both* axes (information gain deficits of 0.9–1.5 nats/event;
PR-AUC 0.146 vs ~0.22). Crucially, we defended against the "strawman ETAS" objection first
— adding an EM-declustered spatially-variable-μ ETAS and an independently-coded Mizrahi
ETAS — so the loss is to *modern* physics, not a toy. The honest headline is therefore
sharper than v1's: not "ML fails to beat ETAS," but "with adequate power, ETAS beats ML,
and the four physics variants are indistinguishable from each other."

**Act 3 — The cautionary tale that makes the method matter.** The most important negative
result is the GNSS channel. By the pre-registered bootstrap rule alone, the GNSS-augmented
hybrid *beats* the plain hybrid (both CIs exclude zero). A less careful paper would have
reported a positive. The placebo battery — time-shuffle, circular-shift, spatial-support —
shows a randomized GNSS series reproduces the same "gain," 60% of which lives in one
window. The channel is a null. This is the paper's rhetorical payload: a confidence
interval that excludes zero is *necessary but not sufficient*; without label-shuffle
controls, block-bootstrap significance can certify structured noise. The GNSS null both
vindicates v1's simpler GNSS null and elevates the whole benchmark's trustworthiness.

## Where the constructive results land
- **CSEP (genuine pyCSEP):** the cascade and sv-ETAS are number- and magnitude-consistent
  with observed seismicity; the first-generation Mizrahi under-counts (no secondary
  triggering). This is the field's minimum bar, cleared with community tooling.
- **sv-ETAS ≈ first-gen** is a *finding*, not a failure: the first-generation background was
  already spatially adaptive (μ CoV 1.27), so EM re-estimation buys nothing here.
- **"More data" does not rescue ML:** a dense sub-Mc3 catalogue and a repeater channel were
  designed and tested under the same gate; neither promoted. Scarcity is not the binding
  constraint — model class is.
- **The useful product:** spatial localization of the 2025 Mw 6.2 was available all year
  (top ~1% cell); timing was foreshock-bounded (42× probability gain ten minutes ahead).
  The deliverable is a standing "where" map with candid "when" limits, and a live 30-day
  regional M≥6 probability near 1%.

## What to foreground vs soften
- **Foreground:** the pre-registered rule; the powered-target physics win against modern
  baselines; the GNSS placebo lesson; genuine pyCSEP consistency.
- **Soften / reframe:** any v1 language implying the cascade is *the best* model — at y35 it
  is one of an inseparable family, and at y30 it is inside the physics cluster, not uniquely
  on top. Drop "ranks best" as a standalone claim.
- **Do not overclaim** transfer to the wider North Anatolian Fault: the catalogue and
  geometry are Marmara-only, so NAF-wide generalization is stated as untested
  (`results/transfer_naf.md`).

## Reviewer-proofing (anticipated objections)
1. *"Your ETAS is a strawman."* → sv-ETAS + independent Mizrahi + first-gen, all inseparable.
2. *"Row bootstrap would be tighter."* → intended; row-level ignores window correlation and
   gives fake-tight CIs (block CI ≈ 7× wider; audit in `v3_bootstrap_audit.json`).
3. *"You buried the GNSS positive."* → the opposite: we reported the CI-positive and then
   killed it with placebos; this is the headline method result.
4. *"pyCSEP was faked / in-house only."* → genuine pyCSEP 0.8.0, N/M agree with in-house for
   all three models; environment caveat (exFAT vs APFS) documented.
